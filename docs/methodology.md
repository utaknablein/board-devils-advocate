# Methodology

The Board Devil's Advocate works because it is predictable. Every proposal gets the same challenge, in the same shape, regardless of who sponsors it. This document describes that shape.

## When it runs

The challenge runs once the board pack is locked, typically five to seven working days before the meeting. Running earlier invites management to pre-edit around it; running later leaves directors no time to use it. The company secretary (or board office) submits the proposal, and the brief is distributed to directors with the pack. Management sees the brief at the same time as the board, not before.

## The eight lenses

### 1. Premise
Lists what must be true for the proposal to deliver, then separates what is **evidenced** from what is **asserted**. Most weak proposals are not wrong; they simply carry one load-bearing assumption nobody has tested.

### 2. Outside view
Places the decision in its reference class. How do acquisitions, platform migrations, market entries or automation programs of this kind usually turn out? If the proposal implies it will beat the typical outcome, the lens asks on what grounds. The challenger labels general patterns as general and does not fabricate statistics.

### 3. Evidence
Traces the key numbers to their source. Were they produced or validated by anyone whose fee, bonus or target depends on approval? Is the sample recent, representative and complete?

### 4. Alternatives
Names the options that did not reach the board. Boards are often shown one option against "do nothing." The lens looks for the phased version, the partnership, the smaller bet and the delay.

### 5. Downside
Describes the worst plausible outcome and how reversible the decision is. A reversible decision with a bad downside is very different from an irreversible one.

### 6. Incentives
Asks who benefits from approval and where the urgency comes from. This is not an accusation. Incentives are normal; unexamined incentives are the problem.

### 7. Execution
Tests capacity. Who owns delivery, what else are they running, and what quietly gets deprioritized to make room?

### 8. Pre-mortem
Assumes failure two years out and writes the most likely story. This surfaces risks that feel impolite to raise as predictions but easy to raise as a hypothetical.

## Severity

Each lens is rated:

- **High**: if this is wrong, the proposal's core case fails or the downside is material and hard to reverse.
- **Medium**: a real gap that should be closed or conditioned, but the case could survive it.
- **Low**: worth noting; unlikely to change the decision on its own.

Overall **challenge intensity** (High, Elevated, Moderate) describes how much scrutiny the proposal warrants. It is not a verdict on whether the proposal is good.

## The brief

Every brief has the same five parts:

1. **Headline**: the single most important thing the board should test.
2. **Eight findings**, each with a severity and an **"ask in the room"** question a director can use verbatim.
3. **Conditions** a prudent board might attach if it approves.
4. **What would change this analysis**: the evidence that would weaken the challenge. This keeps the challenger honest and gives management a constructive path.
5. A standing note that the challenger does not recommend approval or rejection.

## What it deliberately does not do

- It does not vote, score proposals for approval, or rank sponsors.
- It does not comment on individuals' competence or character.
- It does not replace legal, audit or regulatory review.
- It does not see board deliberations, only the paper.
